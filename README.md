# BIsland — Boston Identity Island

A desktop-first, single-player prototype of a browser-based 3D office for a team of eight. Built with React, TypeScript, React Three Fiber, Drei, Three.js, and Vite. All scenery is generated from geometry; no external 3D models are needed.

## Run

Use Node.js 24 LTS and npm.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite. Production build: `npm run build`. Preview the build: `npm run preview`.

## Explore

- Click the ground to walk around obstacles. The island is an office park on the water (about 78 × 65 units; `GROWTH` in `src/shoreline.ts` sets its size, and each zone keeps its own layout while moving outward with the coast): an arrival pier and glass lobby on the south shore, a central plaza with a fountain and team-news screen, a main workspace with 14 desks in Design and Engineering areas around a planted atrium, Pavilions S, M and L (2, 6 and 12 seats), a focus library with six booths, a café with a sea-view terrace, a town hall amphitheater, a wellness garden with a walking loop around the pond, a boardwalk with loungers on the east beach, and a marked future plot. Buildings have a solid base, a glass window band and roofs (a green roof on the workspace); the lobby and pavilions are glass between timber posts with translucent roofs. Walls block walking, so enter through the doorways where the walkways arrive. When you step inside, or pan the camera onto a building, its roof fades away and the camera-facing walls turn see-through, so you can see the people at their desks. Name boards mark each entrance, and the plaza screen shows the clock and the latest team news. Sitting in a library booth switches your status to Focused; standing up restores it.
- Use WASD or arrow keys to move relative to the camera. On a touch screen, hold the stick at the lower left and drag to walk.
- The camera follows your avatar by default. Choose **Island overview** to see the full island, or **Follow me** to return. Right-drag to orbit; use the on-screen + / − buttons to zoom. On a touch screen, drag to orbit and pinch to zoom; a short tap still walks or uses what’s under your finger. Reset view restores the camera orientation and zoom for the selected mode.
- Click a chair to sit; walk or select **Stand up** to leave.
- Select any of nineteen places in the scrollable sidebar to travel there. A round mini-map in the corner stays centred on you (north up) with an arrow for the way you face, the place icons, coworkers and the route you're walking. Click it or press **M** for the full map: both islands with place names, trails, the bridge and the water-taxi route, and where everyone on the team is (names step aside when people sit close together). On the full map, click to walk there (the route is drawn), double-click to jump, drag and scroll to look around, and use Office / Highlands / Both to frame it. Its side panel lists the trails (with length, difficulty and a ✓ once done, plus a Hike button) and people (Find). Esc closes it. Travelling or starting to walk restores Follow me.
- Check in or out, change availability, and edit your name and avatar color.
- You never lose yourself behind scenery: when trees, roofs, or canopies cover your avatar, a tinted silhouette shows through them, and a soft ring in your color pulses at your feet (also shown when zoomed far out). Coworkers get a fainter silhouette. Scenery itself never fades or changes.
- Take a break: ping-pong (with rallies) and cornhole on the games lawn; hammocks that sway and a firepit with log seats by the east beach; a fishing jetty off the west coast where something bites every so often, with kayaks that paddle out on one of three trips (see Rides); a piano that plays a soft improvisation while you sit at it; a lo-fi radio on the café counter you hear louder the closer you are; loungers on the workspace's rooftop terrace (up the outdoor stair); a team quiz at golden hour and movie night at dusk on the town hall screen; and a telescope by the yoga deck that shows a constellation after dusk and spots whales and dolphins by day.
- Rides (all on autopilot; any movement key brings you back): from the water sports dock on the south-west shore, a jet ski that hops waves, a sailboat for two, and parasailing behind a tow boat that lifts you 10 units up while the camera pulls back over the island. Each boat has a long list of named trips (15 each for the sailboat, jet ski and kayak, 10 for the parasail) and picks one as you board, so a ride is rarely the same twice; the message when you set off says where you're going and how. Besides the trips close to home (laps of the island, the strait under the rope bridge, the reef and the wreck, round both islands, the Highlands' jetty, coastal ovals for the parasail), every boat makes voyages to named parts of the open sea, spread over all of it: the south banks, the south-west deep, the south-east shoals, the far west and far east, the western and eastern reaches, the sea either side of the Highlands, and the north-west corner, the open sea north of the Highlands and the north-east corner. Voyages go straight out when the way is clear and round the outside of the islands when it isn't, and each one sails a different pattern: once round, a figure of eight, a weave all the way out, a spiral in and out, three marks like a regatta course, a clover leaf, one long sweep, or a full circuit of both islands. Long voyages are made at a brisker pace so no ride runs much past four minutes. Parasail routes all stay well clear of the rope bridge. If a whale or a pod of dolphins is within reach when a boat sets off, the trip bends out to pass where the animal will be (whales first), keeping a respectful distance and clear of land. From the jetty, kayaks and the scuba dive (below). On the north shore, a climbing bluff with a lookout on top.
- Scuba diving: the dive ladder by the dive shop on the fishing jetty starts a guided dive over the coral reef and out to a shipwreck further offshore. Once you're under, the view goes underwater: the camera drops to look along the seabed, with sand, rippling light, sunbeams and drifting specks (and a torch at night). W A S D takes over from the tour and swims you anywhere in the dive area; a tank lasts three minutes, then you swim back to the ladder on your own (or press Surface). A manta ray patrols between the reef and the wreck, silversides school in the hold, a chest lies beside it, and a pair of dolphins comes to circle you. Whatever you swim close to goes in your dive log (🤿 in the footer), eleven entries in all, with a note in the Activity feed the first time.
- The Highlands: a wild second island across the strait to the north, reached by a high rope bridge from beside the games lawn (boats pass underneath) or by the water taxi from the water sports dock to its west jetty. A 17-unit mountain with a summit cairn, flag and benches; a lake ringed with birches, with a jetty and lily pads; a creek down from a waterfall pool, with a crystal cave beside the falls; a gorge with a hanging bridge; pine forest, palms by the beach, a ranger hut and a campsite with tents and a campfire. Three marked trails start at the trailhead board: 🟢 Lakeside Loop (easy), 🔵 Waterfall Trail (moderate) and ⚫ Summit Ridge (hard, in switchbacks). Pick one on the board to hike it on autopilot. Passing every checkpoint earns that trail's badge, and the footer counts trails done and summits this week. Deer graze in the clearings and bolt if you come close, birds lift off the trees, fireflies come out at night and a cloud bank wraps the mountain in the morning. The team hikes too: lake loops, lunchtime walks to the falls and summit climbs, and Wilson heads over to the campsite fire at dusk.
- What you can do, where you are: walk up to a place and buttons appear over its name for everything to do there ("🎣 Fish · 🛶 Kayak · 🤿 Dive" at the jetty), one per kind of thing; click one to walk to the nearest free spot and start. From a little further off the name shows just the icons, and from afar only the name. Only the place you're at opens, a button is greyed out while every spot of its kind is in use, and it all stays hidden while you're seated, on a ride, diving or on autopilot.
- Zoom: scroll (or + / −) from close-up all the way out to the whole sea with both islands in it, which is as far as the view goes; place names hide once you're far enough out that they'd pile up.
- Small touches: Mochi the island cat wanders between sunny spots and follows you for a while after you pet her; Limo the island dog (a small white Yorkshire terrier with a long silky coat) trots between favourite spots, turns to you with tail going when you come close, and tags along after a pat; five seashells wash up along the coast each day (walk over them to collect); emotes (wave, dance, high five, cheer) that nearby coworkers react to; a nudge to take a break after a long stretch at your desk; and passing showers followed by a rainbow (or bring one on from the island settings).
- The team uses the fun spots too: piano and hammock breaks, ping-pong and cornhole at lunch, fishing in the morning, the rooftop at golden hour, and movie night or the firepit at dusk. Everyone takes rides as well (kayaks, jet skis, sailing, parasailing, the climbing bluff and reef dives), at least three a day each. One-seat rides are never shared: if someone is already on it, the planner picks another errand. Take the other sailboat seat to sail with whoever is aboard.
- Autopilot when you step away: after 90 seconds with no input while you're checked in (a 3-second countdown shows first), and not while you're out on a ride (the clock starts once you're back), your avatar carries on with a day of its own, planned like a coworker's. It works at your desk in the morning, sits in on meetings at midday, focuses in the library at golden hour and watches the movie at dusk, with coffee runs, the piano, rides (kayak, jet ski, parasail, climbing, diving, a sunset sail) and hikes in between. If you were sitting somewhere when you went idle, that seat becomes home. Focused keeps you at a desk or the library and In a meeting keeps you in a pavilion. A pill at the top shows what it's doing, your name tag and the people tab show 🤖, and its outings appear in the Activity feed. Looking around doesn't interrupt it: zooming, dragging to pan, right-dragging to orbit and the Follow me / Island overview buttons leave it running. Any key, click or tap takes back control at once: you stop where you are (a seat or a ride under way stays as it is, and a movement key steps off). The countdown waits while a dialog or the full map is open or you're typing. Turn it off or change the delay (10 s, 30 s, 1 min, 90 s, 5 min) in profile settings, or pause it with the robot button by your status. Fish, trails and summits earned on autopilot count toward your totals.

- Walkways run from the plaza to every zone. The fountain, tables, chairs, benches, stools, loungers, booth panels, lamp posts, flower beds, trees and the pond all block walking. To sit, select a seat; standing up (or walking off) steps you out to the seat's free side, and coworkers walk to that spot before they sit. Palms ring the coast and a few shade trees stand on the lawns; all trees use fixed geometry and never change detail or visibility.

Broad highlights respond to the sun or moon direction; underwater light patterns soften at night. Water colors follow all six day periods, with warmer highlights at sunset and cooler glints at night.

- The open sea: everything else roams the whole sea, round both islands. Each animal rises from the deep at one edge, crosses on a curved route that keeps clear of land, sinks away at another edge and comes back on a new route, so the sea is different every time you look (the clock starts from the time of day). - Signs on the surface and by time of day: splash rings spread wherever something leaves or re-enters the water. Whales breach most at dawn and golden hour and rarely at night. This is a local prototype, with no authentication, shared presence, attendance backend, chat, microphone capture, or voice calls. The meeting areas are scenery for this milestone.

## Hosting the island

Another React app can show the island with its own people. `src/island-office.tsx` exports `IslandOffice`; without props it is the standalone app above, and with a `host` (types in `src/host.ts`) it becomes that app's office:

- `host.people` are the teammates on the island (only those with `present`); each gets a home work seat from their id and a day of short errands (`src/hosted-people.ts`). People who join or leave while the island is open come and go without disturbing anyone else.
- `host.me` supplies your name, full look (hair, hairstyle, skin, shirt and bottoms) and whether you are checked in. Missing look fields fall back to the default avatar. The island's check-in button calls `host.onCheckIn`; clicking a teammate calls `host.onPerson`.
- `host.hour` sets the time of day while the atmosphere follows real time, and `host.weather` (`'clear'` or `'rain'`) owns the sky. While the host sends weather, the manual “Bring a passing shower” control stays off.
- Every style lives under `.island-root`, which fills its container (give it at least 680px of height) and sizes itself from that box. Set `--island-font` and `--island-display` on a parent to use the host's fonts; the web fonts are loaded only by the standalone `index.html`.
- Keys only reach the island when nothing is focused or focus is inside it, so typing elsewhere on the host page never moves your avatar.

`http://127.0.0.1:5173/?hosted` shows a small made-up host page for trying this out. `src/main.tsx`, `src/app.tsx` and `src/standalone.css` belong to the standalone app only.

## Project structure

- `src/main.tsx`: entry point.
- `src/app.tsx`: app shell: header, island panel overlays, bottom bar, and app-wide state.
- `src/sidebar.tsx`, `src/dialogs.tsx`: sidebar (check-in, places, people, activity feed) and the help, profile and atmosphere dialogs.
- `src/scene.tsx`: the 3D world: camera modes, avatar movement, seating, visibility ring, and scene composition.
- `src/scene-parts.tsx`: shared scene pieces (boxes, cylinders, palms, seats by kind, place labels).
- `src/label.tsx`: DOM labels pinned to 3D positions (name tags, place labels).
- `src/use-coworkers.ts`: demo coworker state: routines, work progress, team feed, greetings, and seat ownership.
- `src/profile.ts`: your stored name, color, status, and check-in.
- `src/sea-life.tsx`: geometry-based sea animals and species-specific animation.
- `src/sea-life-motion.ts`: coastal routes and crab behavior.
- `src/open-sea.ts`: open-sea crossings and the timing of pods, whales, schools, flying fish, the shark, the strait turtle and jellyfish (pure functions of time).
- `src/open-sea-scene.tsx`: open-sea animals, spouts, splash rings, shimmer, gulls, bow-riding dolphins and night plankton.
- `src/ocean.tsx`: procedural water gradient, displaced swells, caustics, shoreline foam, and sun/moon highlights in a single shader surface.
- `src/island-life.tsx`: drifting clouds, soft cloud shadows, and evening fireflies.
- `src/daylight.ts`: local time mapping, continuous day-cycle sampling, and saved-settings migration.
- `src/atmosphere.tsx`: lighting presets, atmosphere controls, and synthesized ocean audio.
- `src/shoreline.ts`: shared coastal contour for terrain, collision, ocean effects, and animal routes.
- `src/terrain.tsx`: shaped sand and grass terrain plus click-to-walk surface.
- `src/world.ts`: campus layout (building pads, furniture, seats, walkways, places), collision rules, and A\* navigation (8 directions, no corner cutting) with cached grid checks, straightened into clean lines by line-of-sight smoothing.
- `src/buildings.tsx`: building and pavilion shells (walls between doorways, glass, roofs) that open up when you're inside.
- `src/highlands.ts`, `src/highlands-scene.tsx`: the Highlands as data (coast, terrain height with trails cut into the slope, lake, creek, gorge, bridges and jetties, trails, forest, where you can walk) and in 3D (terrain, water and falls, bridges, trails and markers, hut, camp, cave, summit, instanced forest, deer, birds, fireflies and clouds). `groundHeight(x, z)` is the one source of how high anyone stands.
- `src/batch.ts`, `src/static-batch.tsx`: merges scenery that never moves (seats, desks, palms, campus decor, fun spots, the dock, bluff and reef) into one draw per material; the originals stay for clicks. Mark a moving part inside with `userData={{ dynamic: true }}`.
- `src/boat-trips.ts`: the boats' named trips, built as smooth closed courses kept clear of land and docks, and the wildlife detours.
- `src/rides.ts`, `src/water-sports.tsx`: ride paths over time (shared by the player and the vehicles), and the dock, jet skis, sailboat, tow boat and chute, climbing bluff and dive reef.
- `src/interact.ts`, `src/interaction-hints.tsx`: what can be used and what it does (every seat and ride, the radio, the telescope); the scene shows the action under the pointer with a ring, an `E` prompt on the nearest spot as you walk up, and a marker on every spot while `Q` is held. Place names are drawn as map lettering with an icon; people wear round name tags in their own colour.
- `src/dive.ts`, `src/dive-scene.tsx`, `src/use-dive.ts`: the dive (guided tour path, free swimming, air and the logbook entries), the underwater view, shipwreck and dive shop, and the air gauge and saved dive log.
- `src/fun.tsx`: games lawn, firepit and hammocks, jetty and kayaks, piano, café radio, telescope, rooftop terrace and the town hall screen.
- `src/place-activities.ts`, `src/place-labels.tsx`: what there is to do at each place, which place you're at or near, and the place names that open into buttons.
- `src/island-extras.tsx`: Mochi the cat, Limo the dog, the daily seashells, rain and the rainbow.
- `src/use-fun.ts`, `src/music.ts`, `src/poses.ts`: fun activity state (radio, piano, fishing, shells, emotes, weather, break nudges), generated music, and how avatars sit, lie and paddle.
- `src/campus.tsx`: campus ground plan: plaza and fountain, building floors, furniture, screens, amphitheater, wellness garden, and boardwalk.
- `src/people.ts`: shared person model, demo coworker routines, the home/errand activity planner, and seat/target resolution.
- `src/work-props.tsx`: live desk monitor screens and the meeting whiteboard.
- `src/coworkers.tsx`: shared avatar body and the demo coworker's walking, idle animation, and reactions to you.
- `src/steering.ts`: smooth path following (corner rounding, ease in/out, gradual turning) shared by you and coworkers.
- `src/island-map.tsx`: the round mini-map (centred on you) and the full map (both islands, names, trails, people; click to walk, double-click to jump). Both move markers every frame without re-rendering React.
- `src/style.css`: responsive layout and visual styling.
- `tests/navigation.test.mjs`: boundary, obstacle, destination, and seating accessibility checks.

## Verification

```sh
npm test
npm run format:check
npm run build
```

The navigation tests use Node's built-in TypeScript support and need no installed dependencies. They verify collision boundaries, connections between every named location, access to every seat, and rejection of invalid destinations.

Verification: all 110 tests and the production build (including TypeScript checking) passed. Tests cover exact fivefold area growth, fixed dock coastline, obstacle boundaries, every location and seat, clear district trails, map destinations, day-cycle continuity, ten minutes of sea animal routes, hosted avatar looks, graphics quality, hosted weather, and touch steering.

Browser checks cover district travel through the mini-map, live position updates, camera following while walking, desktop/mobile overview framing, and static tree rendering across camera modes. The atmosphere presets, water shader, motion pause/resume, decoration pointer passthrough, settings persistence, and mobile layout have also been checked. The audio engine was exercised; subjective sound quality was not assessed.

Google Fonts are optional; local sans-serif fallbacks work offline. The 3D scene requires WebGL. A rendering error displays a reload message.
